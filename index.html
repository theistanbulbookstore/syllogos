Digital Catalog of Publications and Public Lectures (1861 - 1915)

Note: The original index for Syngramma Periodikon, the scholarly periodical of the Hellenic Philological Society of Constantinople, gives author names primarily in the genitive form (Ζωγράφου/Zografou rather than Ζωγράφος/Zografos). The index for the Society's public lectures, on the other hand, largely employs the nominative. The catalog below normalises names to the nominative only when they appear in both the nominative and the genitive across the two indices. If looking for Zografos/Ζωγράφος, it is easiest to search for the stem Zograf/Ζωγράφ. The Greek-language search is agnostic to accent marks.

Also note that the Syngramma Periodikon frequently published documents pertaining to the internal workings of the Society (meeting reports, updates on donations, etc.) and book excerpts. These are included only selectively in the 1922 cumulative index this project is based on. If researching such material, please refer to the actual volumes.

      About the Project

      Welcome to the digital repository of the Hellenic Philological Society of Constantinople (Hellenikos Philologikos Syllogos Konstantinoupoleos). Founded in 1861, the Syllogos was the most significant learned society in the Ottoman Empire. It has been called “the Ministry of Education of the Unredeemed Greeks” in the Eastern Mediterranean.

      Amongst its many activities, the Syllogos published a journal and organized public lectures on issues of scholarly and popular interest. The present repository includes a searchable catalogue of both the journal and the public lectures. It also offers various tools to organise, visualise, and analyse this data in ways that were not previously possible.

      The journal articles catalogued here (from 1864 to 1914) are available in hard copy, across thirty-three volumes. Digitized versions of some may be accessed here. The ultimate hope of this project is to fully integrate the text of each article into the repository.

      The texts of the public lectures are lost. In some cases, the speakers developed these into subsequent journal articles, or published them independently. The titles alone give many insights into intellectual and social life in late Ottoman Istanbul, and amongst Ottoman Greeks.

      For each entry, a photograph of the original hard-copy catalog page is also available (under 'SCAN' to the far right of the metadata table). This project is still in development, please do confirm your findings against these as necessary.
